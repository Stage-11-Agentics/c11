c11 tab sheet, round five

Drag the width slider to see a small window, then a big one. Click the count cell (N ▾) to open the list. Hover a row and watch its tab in the bar; hover a tab while the list is open and watch its row.

Size: the list belongs to its area

  • The list is exactly as wide as its area, flush under the bar on both edges, like a drawer pulled out of that area. It never overhangs into a neighbour, except that a very narrow area still gets a 320pt minimum.
  • Columns drop out by width, the same way the tab bar already collapses. Columns are fixed within each width tier, so nothing jitters.
    ≥ 820 everything · 600–819 one clock · 440–599 the agent moves to line 2, clocks go · < 440 Tab N, title, status.
  • Palette steps back: the count cell is bar-colored, not black, so it reads like part of the bar and only goes gold while open. The sheet is #1d1e23.
  • Tabs stay visible longer: the strip scrolls horizontally, with edge fades showing more tabs, instead of folding into the block. It only collapses when fewer than 150pt remain for tabs. A vertical wheel or two-finger scroll over the strip scrolls it sideways. The count cell stays, for the full list.

The relationship: one set of tabs, two orientations

  • Shared vocabulary. Same mark, same Tab N, same order, and the same gold for the visible tab in both the bar and the list.
  • Linked hover (A, B, C). Hovering a row lights its tab in the bar with a white underline, and vice versa. Your eye always knows which tab a row means.
  • Fast unroll: one axis only. The sheet slides down out of the bar in 120ms (clip plus a 6pt translate), and rolls back up in 90ms. There's no per-tab motion, so it's GPU-composited and CPU-light.
  • Count cell: the attention dot, a bold chevron, then the number. The dot's slot is always reserved, so nothing shifts when it appears.
  • Rail (a setting for experts): the count cell slides a vertical list in from the area's left edge. The default stays browser-style tabs.
