C11H-221 · A minimal sidebar for laptops

Decision, 2026-11-05 20:50 PST: Atin chose 04 Two-line card ("definitely wins; love the pop-out"), with one column of mini tiles named as the same shape. Ledger second; Rail and Glyphs too minimal (the title must show). Second round (21:05): a flagged workspace gets a band in the flag's color across its second line; the hover card stays open as you move into it, lists up to 15 agents with panel numbers, and a click jumps to that panel; the switch is by overflow, not width: full cards while they all fit, the compact card the moment they would scroll.

Five prototypes of the c11h sidebar built around what carries the signal: the workspace number, the per-agent marks (blue working, amber waiting, delegate baselines, cold lines) and the counts. Each page shows its proposal in a 1470 × 956 pt window, the size of a 14-inch MacBook Pro, beside today's sidebar at the same height with the same 21 workspaces. Dark and light; hover any row for the full card.

01 · KEY 1

Ledger

Rests on the row. A 26 pt line holds the number, the name (dim; the signal is elsewhere), every agent as a mark sorted flag → waiting → working → idle → cold, and the terminal count. Twenty rows fit with room to spare.

02 · KEY 2

Rail

Rests on the dock. The sidebar becomes a rail of 64 × 36 tiles: the number top-left, the terminal count top-right, up to five marks below with a +N tail. The name lives in the hover flyout. Three times the terminal width of today's sidebar on a laptop.

03 · KEY 3

Mini tiles

Rests on Tiles, the app's existing two-column grid, with a 44 pt tile instead of the full card. Two columns at 255 pt means the sidebar button's cycle (hidden → 1 → 2 → 3) already fits; the compact tile is what makes two columns possible at laptop width.

04 · KEY 4

Two-line card

Rests on the card. Keeps the name at full weight and the count cluster (agents · waiting · terminals) that today's census carries, drops mission, readout, branch, status pill, ports and the rail. About five times today's density; the closest thing to today.

05 · KEY 5

Glyphs

Rests on the claim that the names are the least useful element. Each row is the workspace number and its agents as 11 pt marks, so a whole fleet reads as one column of glyphs. Half today's width and twice Ledger's density. If this is too far, it says where the floor is.

Kept on every proposal

  • The marks, in priority order: flagged, waiting, working, idle, cold. Overflow drops from the quiet end, so the +N tail never hides an amber mark.
  • The counts that matter: agents, waiting, terminals (browsers and docs count as terminals in the small cluster). Fixed slots, tabular numerals, dim at zero, so nothing moves.
  • Selection as a full-perimeter ring, waiting as an amber number, a flag as a violet number, the workspace's own colour as its outline. No left-edge strips.
  • The Waiting Agent jump, compact. Folders as a 20 pt header line.

Questioned

  • The name: dim in Ledger and Mini tiles, full weight in Two-line, absent in Rail and Glyphs. Press N on any page.
  • The mission line, the −% −MB readout, the branch, the status pill, the ports, the PR rows and the composition rail: all moved to hover.
  • The close × on hover: not drawn in any minimal mode (⌘W, the context menu and middle-click remain).
