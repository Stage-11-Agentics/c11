WHERE IT LIVES

A fourth drawer tab, Conversations, between Sent and Insights. A conversation holds both directions, so it belongs beside Received and Sent rather than inside either. Its list is an overview of its own: who this panel talks to, who is sitting on whose mail. A tab also leaves Received, the default, exactly as it is. A click on a sender name elsewhere can open the same thread later (question 2).

THE LIST

One row per peer, newest activity first. Each row is three fixed lines: the peer's panel number (gold, as on the panel bar) and name, with ? for mail that carries no sender stamp; the time of the last message; its first line, with an arrow for direction; whether this panel's last send was taken, is waiting, or its copy is gone; and the peer's unread count. A peer whose panel is closed, or that is known by name only, is dimmed. Rows never change height, so counts can move without the list jumping.

THE THREAD

Oldest at the top, so a reply reads under its parent; a thread opens at its first unread message, otherwise at the bottom. The peer is on the left, this panel on the right. A received message carries UNREAD (this panel's agent has not taken it) or TAKEN. A sent message carries the state of the peer's copy: WAITING, TAKEN or GONE. An unread run is outlined in orange behind a divider. A reply chip jumps to its parent and outlines it. Bodies longer than 12 lines fold behind Show all; a file body shows its path and size; a sent message whose copy is gone keeps its topic, time and recipients. Day separators use local time. When the peer's side goes back further than this panel's sent record, a dashed marker says where the record starts.

WIDTHS

At 900 and 620 the drawer shows the list and the open thread side by side (list 280 or 220 wide). At 360 it shows the list, then the thread with a back control; Esc also goes back. Both panes are a fixed height, the drawer's maximum, so opening a different thread never resizes the drawer.

READ-ONLY

There is no compose box. The drawer never sends, claims or deletes mail, and opening a thread marks nothing taken, because the states describe the agents, not the operator. The strip at the foot, where a compose box would sit, says so. Agents reply with c11 mailbox send.

HOW IT WORKS WITH REAL DATA

Received sideThis panel's inbox root (unread) and _read/ (taken). Mail sent with c11 mailbox send from a live panel in this workspace carries ext.c11_sender_panel_id, so it belongs to that peer panel.
Name-only mailOlder mail, raw writers, --from overrides and other workspaces carry only from, a name. It gets its own conversation keyed by that name and is never merged into a panel's, because a name is not proof of a sender.
Sent sideThis panel's _sent/ ledger: one entry per send with id, time, to, topic and recipient panels, never the body. It keeps the newest 300 entries. The body is read from the recipient's copy (inbox root = waiting, _read/ = taken). c11 never deletes a copy, so a copy is gone only if something else removed it; the bubble then says "copy gone".
GapsSends from before the ledger existed, without the CLI's sender claim, to another workspace, or older than the newest 300 have no entry. Those threads show only the peer's side for that stretch; the dashed marker shows where the sent record starts.
BroadcastsOne send to several panels (role:reviewer, or a shared name) is one ledger entry with several recipients. It appears in each recipient's thread with every recipient's state, and its body survives while any copy remains. A broadcast this panel received looks like any single message: only the sender's ledger knows the other recipients.
Repliesin_reply_to holds the parent's envelope id. Received files and ledger entries are both named by that id, so a reply links to its parent in either direction when the parent is in the thread; otherwise the chip shows the id and is not a link.
CostThe list reuses the Insights scan: the newest 5,000 received headers, cached by file name, plus the ledger. That cached header needs one more field, the sender stamp. Full envelopes are read only for the open thread.
Verdict: the data supports it. No new files and no new writes. The caveats are the gaps above: a stretch of a thread can show one side only, name-only mail stays apart from its panel, and a received broadcast cannot show its other recipients.

QUESTIONS

  1. Placement. Ship Conversations as a fourth tab, with Received still the default? Recommended: yes. A conversation spans both directions, so it sits beside Received and Sent rather than inside either.
  2. Click-through. Should a sender in Received, and a sender or recipient in Insights, also open its conversation? Recommended: yes, as a second step. Once the thread exists, one click from "who sent this" to "what have we said" is cheap.
  3. Name-only mail. Keep unstamped mail in its own "name only" row instead of merging it into the panel that has that name now? Recommended: keep it separate. from is unverified, and the drawer already refuses to guess a sender from it.
  4. Broadcasts. Show a broadcast in every recipient's thread, with all recipients' states? Recommended: yes. Replies come back one to one and point at the broadcast, so each thread needs it as the parent.
  5. Sent vocabulary. Rename the Sent tab's UNREAD and READ to WAITING and TAKEN, as here and on the Insights drill-down tiles? Recommended: yes. UNREAD on a sent message reads as if this panel had not read it.
  6. Unread marker in Received. The Received card marks unread mail with a 3 px orange strip on its left edge. Switch it to the full orange outline used here? Recommended: yes. The rules of taste rule out left-edge strips; the outline carries the same signal.
  7. Sent record size. Keep the ledger at the newest 300 sends? Recommended: keep 300 and watch real threads. The marker already shows where the record starts; raise the limit only if busy panels read one-sided.
