Why it looks like this

  1. One concept per page (Atin, decision 1). Three concepts do not fit the 600 pt sheet as one screen without becoming a wall of text. Each page keeps four numbered points, the house pattern from C11H-127.
  2. The must-know comes first. Page 1 is the behavior change that can lose work (closing a panel ends its jobs). Close is always one click away; nothing later is required reading.
  3. Nothing jumps. Every page in an audience shares the tallest page's height; buttons keep their places; the counter uses tabular figures.
  4. The window behind teaches too. The mock shows a machine label (STUDIO), one agent mailing another, and a mailbox cell: the things the pages name.

c11 2.0

Your terminals keep running.
Wherever they run.

c11 is a macOS terminal multiplexer for running many agents at once. In 2.0, herdr-core keeps your terminals running past the app, terminals on a server or another Mac you link work the same as local ones, and your agents mail each other. All of it in one window.

What's new in 2.0

Terminals that outlive the app

A helper called hc holds every terminal, so shells and agents keep running when c11 quits, updates or crashes. Closing a panel ends what it started. After a reboot, each saved agent conversation resumes once.

Persistent terminals →

Linked machines

Link a Mac or an x86-64 Linux machine you reach over SSH, and its terminals sit in this window. Launches ask where to run; each title bar names the machine. Link only machines you trust.

Other machines →

Agent mail

Every panel has a mailbox. Agents hand off work and report back with c11 mailbox send; mail arrives when the agent is idle, never mid-turn, and survives a relaunch.

Mail →

Updating from c11 1.x

  • Quitting no longer ends your terminals. Settings ▸ General ▸ Keep Terminals After Quit turns that off.
  • Closing a panel ends everything it started, background jobs too. The close button says End Session.
  • Your workspaces, panels and settings carry over. The first launch explains the rest once.

Trust and privacy

  • c11 sends no crash reports or analytics, whatever the setting says. c11 1.8.1 sent them by default; 2.0 does not.
  • Linking a machine gives c11 a shell there as you. What that means, and what it does not protect: How c11 trusts a linked machine.
  • Agents learn c11 from its skill. c11 guide prints the card for the build you run.
c11 is open source (AGPL-3.0-or-later), built on cmux, Ghostty and Bonsplit. hc is herdr-core (Apache-2.0), bundled unmodified.

What it links to

  • "Updating from c11 1.x" opens the What's New text and the changelog's Before you update.
  • The trust link opens C11H-332's trust page, the same text as c11 guide trust.
